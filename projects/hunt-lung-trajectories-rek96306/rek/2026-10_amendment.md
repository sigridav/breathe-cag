# Amendment to REK, October 2026

*Submitted to REK in Norwegian on 26.10.2026. This is an English translation.*

**Project:** 96306 – Clinical characteristics and long-term outcomes of lung function trajectories from early adolescence to adulthood. The HUNT Lung Study.
**Type of change:** Change of project end date; new protocol version (new research questions and data sources)
**New end date:** 31.12.2030
**Attachment:** Revised research protocol with tracked changes (`../protocol/protocol_2026-10_tracked-changes.docx`)

## Assessment of whether the changes affect risk, burden or benefit for participants

The changes involve no new risk or burden for participants. No new participants are recruited, and no participants will be contacted or examined again because of the changes. The new sub-study uses data already collected in HUNT4 (questionnaires, spirometry and blood samples with leukocyte differential counts) and linkage to central health registries, which is covered by the consent participants gave in HUNT.

The extended registry linkages add more health information per participant and thus somewhat increase the privacy risk. The extension covers linkage to the Norwegian Drug Registry, an extended extract from the Norwegian Patient Registry with all diagnoses, admission and discharge dates and level of care, and updated extracts from the Cause of Death Registry. The risk is managed with the same measures as in the original approval. Data are delivered pseudonymised with a project-specific ID, and the linkage key is held by Statistics Norway with no access for the researchers. Only variables necessary for the purpose are requested (data minimisation), and data are stored on NTNU's secure storage platform (NICE). No data are transferred abroad.

The extended project period means data are stored for longer, but under the same security measures. At the end of the project, data will be deleted or anonymised in line with REK's requirements.

The benefit for the group and for society increases. The project may show whether simple and inexpensive blood tests (eosinophils and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio) can distinguish between people at high and low risk of hospitalisation and death. This could improve risk assessment and allow more targeted follow-up and treatment of people with chronic lung disease, including in primary care. Individual participants will not benefit directly from the project.

## Description of and justification for the changes

### Extension of the project period to 31.12.2030

The project currently ends on 31.12.2026. We apply for an extension to 31.12.2030 for three reasons. First, processing of registry data applications has taken considerably longer than planned, and some registry data were received late in the project period. Second, the current linkage to the Cause of Death Registry covers data only up to and including 2021, i.e. 2–4.5 years of follow-up after HUNT4. This is too short to study long-term outcomes, and updated registry data are needed. Third, the follow-up study of Young-HUNT Lung participants is planned during HUNT5 (2028–2030). It will provide new lung function measurements that are central to the project's main aim on lung function trajectories.

### Protocol change: new sub-study on blood biomarkers

We wish to extend the project with a sub-study on blood biomarkers of systemic inflammation (new aim 3 and new section 3.2.11 in the protocol). The protocol already includes death and hospitalisations for respiratory and cardiovascular causes as outcomes. Biomarkers are also mentioned in the publication plan.

People with similar lung function differ greatly in their risk of exacerbations, hospitalisation and death. Eosinophils and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) capture different types of inflammation, and both can be derived from a routine blood test. We will examine whether these markers, separately and in combination, predict severe COPD exacerbations, hospitalisations and death in adults with chronic airflow limitation compared with people with normal lung function. We will also examine whether the markers add prognostic information beyond lung function and symptoms. The sub-study uses data already collected in the HUNT4 Lung Study, where approximately 15,000 participants have both spirometry and a differential count. It involves no new contact with participants.

### Updated and extended registry linkages

To answer the research questions we request the following:

- **Norwegian Patient Registry:** extended from respiratory diagnoses to all main and secondary diagnoses (ICD-10), including cardiovascular diagnoses, plus admission and discharge dates, level of care and the Charlson comorbidity index. This is needed to distinguish hospitalisations from outpatient contacts, to define cardiovascular hospitalisations and to adjust for comorbidity.
- **Cause of Death Registry:** updated extract with underlying and contributing causes of death for respiratory, cardiovascular and cancer deaths. The registry is listed in the protocol and is now also listed explicitly in the registry overview.
- **Norwegian Drug Registry (Legemiddelregisteret; new; since 2023 it also includes data from the former Norwegian Prescription Database):** dispensed drugs with ATC code, dispensing date and amount/DDD for inhaled medication, systemic corticosteroids, antibiotics and cardiovascular drugs. This is needed because inhaled and systemic corticosteroids affect eosinophil counts, and because courses of prednisolone and antibiotics are used to define moderate exacerbations.
- **Emigration date from the National Population Register** (via HUNT Databank) for correct censoring.

Data are requested from 2008 (NPR), 2004 (Norwegian Drug Registry) or from the start of each registry, up to the latest available date within the project period. The HUNT consent covers linkage to health registries. Data handling follows the original approval.

In addition, errors in the previous protocol version have been corrected and the publication plan has been updated. All changes are marked in the attached protocol.
